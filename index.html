What I can build for you

Three ways to put the right difficulty back in.

AI practice simulations

Role-plays where the other side reacts to what you say, for the conversations people dread: conflict, feedback, a price objection. You rehearse the hard part until it stops being hard.

Grounded in deliberate practice: repetition with immediate, specific feedback.3

E-learning and curriculum design

Courses and learning paths built on retrieval, spacing and worked examples, so people leave able to do the thing, with the slides as the least important part.

Grounded in practice testing and spaced practice, the two highest-rated of ten study techniques.4

Work

AI role-play · workplace conflict

ConflictSim

The problem
Conflict training usually ends in a framework people can recite and can't use when the person across the table is upset.
The friction
No list of options to hide behind. You type what you'd say and the character reacts to it: empathy opens them up, blame shuts them down. Afterwards a second model reads the transcript and scores the conversation you had.
The research
Nonviolent Communication shapes the character logic and the feedback dimensions.5

AI coach · B2B sales training

Selly, AI Sales Coach

The problem
Reps learn frameworks, pass quizzes and still freeze on live calls. Roleplay with a manager is rare, and the social stakes change what people are willing to try.
The friction
Selly pushes back when an answer is vague and quotes the rep's own words in feedback. In Coach mode the scaffolding is minimal; you work for the answer.
The research
Deliberate practice, and a 34-page knowledge base built from primary research before the first line of the prompt.3

LLM evaluation, a sample

How I judge an AI tutor.

Five criteria scored 1 to 4, plus a safety gate. In the sample, a nursing student asks about drug half-life. The answer is correct, friendly and well organized, the kind most reviewers would pass, and it still fails: it does the learner's thinking, states a clinical convention as fact, then slides into dosing advice.

"This also tells you that you can safely give the next dose after 30 hours."
A tutor has no business setting a dose.

About

Instructional designer, LLM evaluator, cognitive scientist.

I went for psychopedagogy because psychology was too into the couch and pedagogy too into the institution; I wanted the part where cognition and behavior meet, which turns out to be a tiny corner of academia.

I've been reading AI output and explaining why it's wrong since 2014, long before the field had a name. That habit, judging what a system produced and writing down exactly why, is the one I bring to every course I design.
